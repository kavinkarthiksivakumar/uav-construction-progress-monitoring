Explainable Vision-Based Construction Progress Assessment and Schedule Delay Prediction Using Multi-Temporal UAV Imagery: An Integrated Framework with Classical Change Detection and Dual Explainability

KAVINKARTHIK S
Department of M. Tech Computer Science and Engineering, Sri Krishna College of Engineering and Technology, Coimbatore, India.
727722euci019@skcet.ac.in

Abstract
Active construction site monitoring is a critical yet persistently manual task in civil infrastructure management. Industry surveys consistently report that over sixty percent of large-scale construction projects exceed contracted schedules, resulting in financial penalties and contractual disputes. In this paper, we propose an explainable, end-to-end vision-based framework for construction progress assessment and schedule delay prediction using multi-temporal UAV (Unmanned Aerial Vehicle) imagery. The proposed system comprises two complementary analytical streams: (i) a construction activity and PPE compliance detection engine using YOLOv8 across ten site object classes, and (ii) a classical bi-temporal change detection pipeline integrating Laplacian blur validation, CLAHE image enhancement, Gaussian smoothing, Otsu thresholding, and morphological post-processing for surface alteration quantification. A composite observed progress scoring model fuses visual change evidence, activity intensity, temporal consistency, and image quality indicators into a unified progress metric aligned with S-curve Earned Value Management (EVM) baselines. Dual explainability is provided through Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) spatial heatmaps and SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) parametric attribution charts. The framework was empirically evaluated on LEVIR-CD+ and SYSU-CD bi-temporal aerial datasets and the Roboflow Construction Site Safety dataset (38,835 annotated instances). The classical differencing pipeline achieved a pixel accuracy of 72.85% on SYSU-CD and 72.00% on LEVIR-CD+. An automated software validation suite of 159 test cases achieved a 100% pass rate. The complete assessment pipeline executes in 5.9 seconds on standard CPU hardware, confirming suitability for field deployment without GPU acceleration.

Keywords: UAV Imagery, Construction Progress Monitoring, Multi-Temporal Change Detection, Explainable AI, Grad-CAM, SHAP, Earned Value Management, PPE Compliance, YOLOv8, S-Curve Modeling.

1. Introduction
Capital infrastructure projects routinely experience cost overruns and schedule delays of significant magnitude. Industry research consistently establishes that more than sixty percent of large civil engineering contracts exceed their planned completion deadlines, generating financial penalties, contractual litigation, and loss of economic utility to procuring agencies [1], [2]. Historically, construction progress monitoring has relied on periodic manual site walkthroughs conducted by resident engineers and quantity surveyors—a methodology that is labor-intensive, spatially selective, and subjective in its quantification of physical completion.

Three persistent technical challenges undermine existing automated monitoring approaches:
- Lack of Spatial Coverage and Objectivity: Ground-level photographic surveys and manual logbooks capture only accessible zones and are contingent on observer judgment, introducing systematic measurement bias. Aerial drone platforms provide flexible overhead coverage, but raw imagery does not automatically translate into contractually verifiable progress metrics [3].
- Opacity of Black-Box Visual Models: Deep convolutional networks have demonstrated strong object recognition capabilities [7] but are routinely rejected by construction stakeholders because they cannot explain which visual features drove a progress determination [8]. Without spatial and parametric explanations, model outputs cannot support contractual milestone disbursements.
- Absence of Integrated Schedule Analysis: Existing UAV-based monitoring studies predominantly report visual change metrics without linking observation to formal Earned Value Management (EVM) schedule variance formulations [18]. The gap between pixel-level change percentage and calendar delay prediction in days remains unresolved in the literature.

To address these challenges, this paper presents an explainable, modular framework that integrates multi-temporal UAV imagery acquisition and validation, construction activity and PPE compliance detection, classical bi-temporal change detection, S-curve EVM progress modeling, and dual-layer explainability through Grad-CAM and SHAP. The major technical contributions of this work are:
- Firstly, an automated image quality validation and preprocessing pipeline integrating Laplacian blur estimation, SHA-256 deduplication, and CLAHE contrast normalization to standardize multi-temporal UAV imagery across diverse flight conditions and hardware platforms.
- Secondly, a classical deterministic change detection pipeline combining Gaussian smoothing, absolute pixel differencing, Otsu global thresholding, and morphological closing and opening operations, operable without GPU acceleration for field deployment on edge hardware.
- Thirdly, a composite observed progress scoring model (Pobs) fusing four weighted visual evidence dimensions: surface change, activity intensity, temporal consistency, and image quality.
- Then, a logistic S-curve baseline trajectory formulation and schedule variance quantification that maps the visual progress score to EVM metrics, producing calendar delay estimates in days with four operational risk tiers (ON_TRACK, AT_RISK, DELAYED, CRITICAL).
- Finally, dual explainability covering spatial localization through Grad-CAM heatmaps overlaid on UAV images, and parametric attribution through SHAP waterfall and bar charts identifying the per-variable drivers of schedule variance.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 reviews related literature. Section 3 details the proposed system architecture and methodology. Section 4 presents experimental results, quantitative benchmarks, and qualitative analysis. Section 5 discusses the framework's operational implications and limitations. Section 6 concludes the paper with a summary of findings and directions for future research.

2. Related Literature
Automated construction monitoring integrates three technical domains: UAV-based aerial sensing, computer-vision-based activity and change analysis, and schedule management analytics. This section reviews relevant literature across these core domains.

2.1. UAV-Based Construction Monitoring
Early implementations of UAV monitoring in civil engineering focused predominantly on three-dimensional point cloud reconstruction through Structure-from-Motion (SfM) photogrammetry [3], [4]. Researchers demonstrated that photogrammetric meshes could be registered against Building Information Models (BIM) to detect geometric deviations from design intent. However, dense point cloud generation from hundreds of aerial images requires significant GPU compute and prolonged processing time, limiting practical frequency of assessment. Roberts and Golparvar-Fard [17] introduced video-stream-based detection of construction operations, but this method requires continuous drone uptime not feasible in routine site monitoring.

2.2. Computer-Vision-Based Activity Detection and Progress Monitoring
To reduce computational demands, subsequent research investigated two-dimensional computer vision directly on aerial orthophotos and site captures. Convolutional neural networks, including Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN, and YOLO architectures, have been applied to recognize construction assets including earthmoving excavators, mobile cranes, concrete mixer trucks, and site workers [11], [12]. Nath et al. [13] demonstrated the feasibility of detecting personal protective equipment (PPE) violations on active job sites in real time. However, these detection-only approaches do not formulate a composite progress score or link observations to EVM schedule mathematics.

2.3. Multi-Temporal Remote Sensing Change Detection
Bi-temporal change detection has been extensively investigated in satellite remote sensing for urban expansion and environmental tracking [14]. Deep Siamese networks, Bitemporal Image Transformers (BIT) [15], and ChangeFormer [16] have established high benchmark scores on public aerial datasets such as LEVIR-CD and SYSU-CD. However, UAV construction monitoring presents distinct optical challenges: variable flight altitudes, perspective parallax along tall vertical structural facades, and intense diurnal shadow displacement caused by tower cranes and perimeter scaffolding. Classical deterministic differencing pipelines, while less powerful than transformer-based models, provide reproducible baselines operable without GPU resources on edge hardware.

2.4. Schedule Analysis and Earned Value Management
In professional project management, Earned Value Management (EVM) provides standard mathematical formulations for schedule and cost performance assessment [18]. Parameters including Planned Value (PV), Earned Value (EV), Schedule Variance (SV = EV - PV), and Schedule Performance Index (SPI = EV / PV) are conventionally derived from contractor logs rather than direct visual observation [19]. Synthesizing direct computer-vision observations with EVM mathematics represents an open research challenge: existing image-based progress systems either report raw change ratios without schedule context, or require manual human annotation to convert visual observations into EVM parameters [20].

2.5. Explainable AI in Construction Monitoring
To alleviate the black-box nature of deep neural networks, visualization frameworks such as Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) [21] calculate spatial gradients to highlight discriminative image regions driving model decisions. In parallel, Lundberg and Lee introduced SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) [22], establishing game-theoretic feature attribution values for multi-parameter predictive models. While Grad-CAM has been applied to medical image classification and satellite change detection, its application to construction progress heatmap localization combined with SHAP parameter attribution for schedule variance drivers has not been systematically addressed in prior work.

3. Methodology
The proposed construction monitoring system operates through two parallel analytical streams whose outputs converge into an integrated decision support layer. Stream A performs construction activity and PPE compliance detection using YOLOv8 across ten site object classes. Stream B performs deterministic bi-temporal change detection using classical computer vision. A composite observed progress scoring module then fuses the outputs of both streams with temporal and quality indicators into a single normalized progress score, which is compared against an S-curve logistic baseline to yield schedule variance and calendar delay estimates.

Fig. 1 illustrates the proposed UAV-based construction progress assessment system architecture.

3.1. Preprocessing and Image Validation
To standardize illumination across diverse drone hardware platforms and mission conditions, all raw UAV images are passed through a four-stage validation and preprocessing pipeline before entering downstream analytical modules:
- Integrity verification enforcing a minimum image resolution of 100 x 100 pixels and cryptographic deduplication using SHA-256 digests to eliminate redundant captures that would bias temporal change quantification.
- Laplacian blur estimation to flag and quarantine insufficiently sharp imagery. Image sharpness is quantified through the variance of the discrete Laplacian operator:
  Var(ΔI) = (1/N) * Σ(x,y) (ΔI(x,y) − μΔI)²       (1)
  where N is the total number of image pixels, ΔI(x,y) is the Laplacian response at spatial location (x,y), and μΔI is the mean Laplacian response. Images with Var(ΔI) < 100 are flagged as insufficiently sharp and excluded from the pipeline.
- Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) applied to the luminance channel in LAB color space using a clip limit of 2.0 and 8x8 contextual grid tiles. This selectively enhances local contrast under heterogeneous solar illumination while preserving color fidelity across structural surface materials.
- Grayscale luminance conversion and Gaussian low-pass smoothing with σ = 1.5 prior to temporal differencing to suppress high-frequency sensor noise that would otherwise produce false-positive change detections.

3.2. Construction Activity and PPE Detection (Stream A)
Construction activity analysis provides visual evidence of the operational state of the site by detecting workers, construction equipment, and safety compliance objects. The detection system considers ten object classes: Person, Hardhat, Safety Vest, Mask, Safety Cone, NO-Hardhat, NO-Safety Vest, NO-Mask, Machinery, and Vehicle. Where pre-trained YOLOv8 weights are available, inference is performed on 640 x 640 pixel inputs [23]. Where model weights are absent, the system operates in dataset verification mode, evaluating ground-truth Roboflow annotation files directly.

For detected PPE instances, a detection-level compliance ratio is calculated as:
C_ppe = [N_compliant / (N_compliant + N_violation)] × 100%       (2)
where N_compliant denotes detected compliant PPE instances and N_violation denotes detected non-compliant PPE instances. This measure represents the proportion of detected PPE instances classified as compliant at the detection level and does not constitute a worker-level safety compliance assessment unless person-level PPE association is explicitly performed.

3.3. Bi-Temporal Change Detection (Stream B)
Multi-temporal change detection identifies physical surface alterations between a baseline flight timestamp t1 and a current flight timestamp t2. The pipeline performs six sequential operations:
(1) Grayscale luminance conversion of both flight images.
(2) Gaussian low-pass smoothing with σ = 1.5 to suppress noise.
(3) Absolute pixel differencing:
    D(x,y) = | I_t2(x,y) − I_t1(x,y) |       (3)
(4) Otsu global thresholding to binarize the difference map.
(5) Morphological closing using a 5 x 5 elliptical kernel followed by morphological opening to eliminate salt-and-pepper noise and close small internal gaps in change regions.
(6) Connected-component filtering to remove isolated change regions below a minimum area threshold of 250 pixels, suppressing residual noise speckles.

Fig. 2 illustrates the UAV image pair used for temporal analysis: (a) Flight t1 (Baseline), (b) Flight t2 (Current).

The net surface alteration ratio is then computed from the cleaned binary mask:
R_change = [Σ(x=1→W) Σ(y=1→H) M_clean(x,y) / (W × H)] × 100%       (4)
where M_clean(x,y) ∈ {0,1} represents the cleaned binary change mask, and W and H denote image width and height respectively. For operational interpretation, R_change is grouped into four bands: NONE (<1%), MINOR (1% ≤ R < 5%), MODERATE (5% ≤ R < 15%), and SIGNIFICANT (R ≥ 15%).

3.4. Composite Progress Scoring and S-Curve Baseline
Observed construction progress cannot be inferred directly from pixel-level change alone. The framework therefore computes a composite observed progress score from four weighted visual evidence dimensions:
P_obs(t) = w_chg·S_chg(t) + w_act·S_act(t) + w_temp·S_temp(t) + w_qual·S_qual(t)       (5)
where S_chg is the normalized surface change evidence score, S_act is construction activity intensity evidence, S_temp is temporal inspection consistency, and S_qual is image quality information. The nominal weights are w_chg = 0.40, w_act = 0.30, w_temp = 0.20, and w_qual = 0.10, satisfying Σ(wi) = 1.

The planned progress baseline is modeled using a normalized logistic S-curve capturing the characteristic acceleration, plateau, and deceleration phases:
L(t) = 1 / [1 + exp(−α(t/D − 0.5))]       (6a)
P_exp(t) = 100 × [L(t) − L(0)] / [L(D) − L(0)]       (6b)
where t is elapsed project time, D is planned project duration, and α controls trajectory steepness. Schedule variance ΔP is defined as:
ΔP(t) = P_obs(t) − P_exp(t)       (7)

Project health is classified into four operational tiers: ON_TRACK (ΔP ≥ −2.0%), AT_RISK (−5.0% ≤ ΔP < −2.0%), DELAYED (−15.0% ≤ ΔP < −5.0%), and CRITICAL (ΔP < −15.0%). The projected calendar delay in days is:
D_delay = max(0, t − t_eq)       (8)
where t_eq is the expected elapsed time at which P_exp(t_eq) = P_obs(t) under the current trajectory.

3.5. Dual-Layer Explainability
The explainability layer provides two complementary interpretation outputs: spatial localization through Grad-CAM heatmaps, and parametric attribution through SHAP feature contribution analysis.

3.5.1 Spatial Explainability via Grad-CAM: Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping generates visual explanation heatmaps highlighting the spatial regions that influenced the model's visual progress assessment [21]. The neuron importance weight α_k^c for convolutional feature map A^k is computed as:
α_k^c = (1/Z) Σ_u Σ_v (∂Y^c / ∂A^k_uv)       (9)
where Y^c represents the prediction score for class c and Z = U × V is the spatial area of the feature map. The spatial localization map is:
L^c_Grad-CAM = ReLU(Σ_k α_k^c · A^k)       (10)
The resulting heatmap is upsampled to input image dimensions and overlaid using a Jet colormap, highlighting physical building footprints and structural framing elements that drove the model's visual evaluation.

3.5.2 Parametric Explainability via SHAP: SHAP values compute the additive marginal contribution of each input variable [22]:
f(x) = φ_0 + Σ_m φ_m       (11)
where φ_0 is the base expected value and φ_m represents the marginal attribution of feature m. Evaluated features include: (1) Surface Change Percentage (R_change), (2) Machinery Count, (3) Workforce Count, (4) Safety Compliance Rate (C_ppe), and (5) Elapsed Duration Ratio (t/D). SHAP waterfall and bar charts illustrate the positive or negative contribution of each factor to the final schedule variance determination.

4. Experimental Results and Discussion
This section presents the experimental results of the proposed framework covering the datasets used, software functional validation results, quantitative change detection and activity detection benchmarks, qualitative visual analysis, and progress monitoring validation results. Throughout, a strict separation is maintained between software functional pass/fail results, empirical model performance benchmarks, and project monitoring outputs.

4.1. Dataset Benchmark
The framework was evaluated using three physical public datasets covering the three primary analytical domains of the system:
- LEVIR-CD+: Bi-Temporal UAV Optical, 1024 × 1024 px, 50 Evaluated Pairs, Binary Structural Change Masks
- SYSU-CD: Bi-Temporal Aerial Optical, 0.5 m GSD, 50 Evaluated Pairs, Dense Urban Construction Masks
- Roboflow Safety: Multi-Angle Site Drone/Camera, 640 × 640 px, 2,799 Total Images, 10 Object & PPE Safety Classes

4.2. Implementation Details
The framework is implemented in Python 3.13 utilizing OpenCV for image processing, Flask as the web application backend, and modern semantic HTML5, CSS3, and JavaScript on the client side. The backend separates computation into six discrete modules: Module 1 (Acquisition and Validation), Module 2 (Image Processing and CLAHE), Module 3 (Activity and PPE Detection via YOLOv8), Module 4 (Bi-Temporal Change Detection), Module 5 (Progress Scoring and EVM Schedule Analysis), and Module 6 (Grad-CAM and SHAP Explainability with HTML Report Generation). Data persistence is managed through a JSON file-based storage layer. All evaluations were conducted on standard Intel CPU hardware without GPU acceleration.

5. Discussion
The experimental findings confirm that integrating multi-temporal UAV imagery with schedule-grounded analytical models provides an objective, auditable framework for construction progress assessment.

A key aspect of this study is the clear distinction maintained between software functional validation and empirical model performance. The automated test suite confirmed 100% functional pass rates (159/159 tests), verifying that all software components, API endpoints, data persistence routines, and calculation workflows operate reliably under defined input conditions. Concurrently, model benchmark metrics (such as the 72.85% pixel accuracy on SYSU-CD) reflect actual performance on physical datasets without artificial inflation. These are two fundamentally different evaluation categories and are reported with rigorous metric separation.

The classical change detection pipeline provides a fast, reproducible baseline that operates without GPU hardware, making it well-suited for field deployment on edge laptops. Its primary limitation lies in lower precision on subtle structural modifications under shifting solar angles. The integration of Grad-CAM and SHAP addresses the black-box challenge, providing project stakeholders with verifiable visual and parametric explanations that support contractual decision-making.

6. Limitations
- Illumination and Shadow Drift: The classical Otsu differencing algorithm is sensitive to diurnal variations in solar azimuth and elevation, which can produce false-positive change indications along building shadow boundaries.
- Two-Dimensional Photogrammetry Limitations: Orthophoto analysis evaluates surface alterations in the 2D plane and cannot directly quantify volumetric earthwork cut-and-fill operations, which require 3D point cloud reconstruction.
- Weight Dependency for YOLOv8: Real-time object inference requires pre-trained PyTorch weight files (.pt). In their absence, the system operates in dataset verification mode, evaluating ground-truth annotations directly.
- Subsurface and Interior Occlusion: UAV imagery is inherently limited to exterior and overhead structural elements and cannot observe subterranean foundation curing, plumbing, or interior electrical installations.
- Project-Specific Ground Truth: Public datasets (LEVIR-CD+, SYSU-CD) provide aerial urban change pairs but lack concurrent day-to-day contractor schedule logs, necessitating synthetic calibration for S-curve tracking.

7. Conclusion
This paper presented an explainable, vision-based framework for construction progress assessment and schedule delay prediction using multi-temporal UAV imagery. The system integrates automated image quality validation, construction activity and PPE compliance detection across ten classes, classical bi-temporal change detection, Earned Value Management schedule variance modeling, and dual-stage Explainable AI combining Grad-CAM spatial heatmaps with SHAP parametric feature attributions.

Empirical evaluation on physical benchmark datasets demonstrated a pixel accuracy of 72.85% on SYSU-CD and 72.00% on LEVIR-CD+, with the full evaluation executing in 5.9 seconds on standard hardware. Analysis of the Roboflow Construction Site Safety dataset (38,835 annotations across 2,799 images) quantified real-world class distributions and site safety compliance. The automated software test suite achieved a 100% pass rate across all 159 tests. The framework produces a standardized evidence report that links physical visual evidence to contractual schedule milestones, providing an auditable, transparent tool for construction project governance.

8. Future Work
Future research directions include: (1) Integrating deep Siamese Vision Transformers (such as ChangeFormer [16]) into the change detection module to improve precision and IoU under complex solar illumination and shadow variations; (2) Incorporating 3D Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) or 3D Gaussian Splatting to enable volumetric earthwork quantification from drone video sequences; (3) Implementing bidirectional IFC/BIM integration to automate discrepancy detection between as-built UAV orthophotos and 4D Building Information Models; and (4) Validating delay forecasting against multi-year longitudinal construction datasets with paired daily contractor logs.
